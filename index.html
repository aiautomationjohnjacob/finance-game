What is a Call Option?

A call option gives the buyer the right (but not obligation) to buy 100 shares at a specific strike price before the expiry date. The seller receives a premium upfront.

Example: Stock at $50. You sell a call option with $55 strike expiring in 30 days, receiving $2.00 premium ($200 total). If the stock stays below $55, you keep the $200 and the option expires worthless.

What is a Put Option?

A put option gives the buyer the right to sell 100 shares at the strike price before expiry. A cash-secured put means you keep enough cash on hand to buy the shares if assigned.

The Wheel Strategy

The wheel strategy combines two steps:

  1. Sell a cash-secured put on a stock you want to own. Collect premium.
  2. If assigned (stock drops below strike), you now own shares. Then sell a covered call above your cost basis.
  3. Repeat — collecting premium each cycle.

Risk: the stock can drop significantly, creating large unrealized losses that exceed the premium collected.

What is a Futures Contract?

A futures contract is a legal agreement to buy or sell a commodity (oil, gold, wheat) or financial instrument at a predetermined price at a future date. Each contract represents a fixed quantity — e.g., 1 E-mini S&P 500 futures contract = 50× the index price.

Leverage & margin: You don't pay the full contract value upfront. You post a margin deposit (typically 5–10% of contract value). This amplifies both profits and losses by 10–20×.

Margin Call Explained

If the market moves against your position and your account equity falls below the maintenance margin, your broker issues a margin call — demanding more funds immediately, or forcibly liquidating your position at a loss.

Warning: Margin calls can result in losses exceeding your initial deposit.
